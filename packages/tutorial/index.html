Connect to a Lightning peer

Lightning nodes talk to each other over raw TCP, encrypted with a handshake called BOLT 8. Your browser cannot open a TCP socket. By the end of this lesson your browser will be a Lightning peer anyway.

Why this is awkward

A browser can open HTTP, WebSocket and WebRTC connections, and nothing else. A Lightning peer listens on TCP port 9735 and expects a byte stream. So something has to sit in the middle and copy bytes between a WebSocket and a TCP socket — that is all the proxy in this lesson does. After the handshake it is carrying ciphertext it cannot read.

What happens on the wire

The connection opens with Noise_XK, a three-message handshake from BOLT 8. You send 50 bytes, the peer replies with 50, you send 66. At the end both sides share keys, and — this is the part that matters — the peer has proved it owns the public key you dialled. There is no certificate authority anywhere in Lightning.

Then both sides send init, listing the features they support as a bitfield. Some bits are required: if your peer requires something you do not understand, it hangs up. Watch the wire pane when you run your code — you will see your init go out and theirs come back.

Your job

LDK is already running in this page: a PeerManager compiled to WASM, inside a Web Worker. It knows the protocol. What it does not have is a way to reach the network — that is WsLdkNet, the WebSocket transport, which is already wired up for you as net.

Write code that opens the connection and waits until the peer is really connected.

What you have

net.connect_link(url, peer_id)opens a connection that reconnects itself; returns a link
link.wait_connected()resolves when the socket is open
net.await_peer(peer_id)resolves when the peer is connected — handshake and init both done
peer_urlthe peer's address, through the proxy
peer_idthe peer's public key, 33 bytes
log(line)prints to the output pane

Those last two are a real distinction, not pedantry. A socket can be open to a proxy that is about to refuse your target — the connection looks fine for a moment, then closes. Waiting for the peer is what tells you Lightning is actually working.
